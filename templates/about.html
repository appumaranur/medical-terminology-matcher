Executive Summary & Problem Statement
Academic AI/ML Research

Medical and clinical terminologies frequently feature dual representation: informal colloquial patient expressions (e.g. "Heart Attack", "High Blood Pressure", "Acid Reflux") alongside formal ICD-10/SNOMED-CT Greco-Latin nomenclatures (e.g. "Myocardial Infarction", "Hypertension", "Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease").

Conventional search engines and lexical similarity metrics (Levenshtein Edit Distance, Jaccard Index, TF-IDF) fail fundamentally when comparing these equivalents because they share zero lexical overlap. This research project presents an industry-grade, end-to-end medical terminology matching system powered by Sentence-BERT (Siamese contrastive MiniLM), BioBERT (PubMed biomedical contextual masked language modeling), and FAISS high-throughput sub-millisecond vector indexing.

Mathematical Formulations
Formal NLP Metrics
1. Cosine Similarity on Unit-Normalized Embeddings

Given two dense vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$, cosine similarity measures angular alignment in high-dimensional semantic space:

$$\cos(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2} = \sum_{i=1}^d \hat{u}_i \hat{v}_i \quad \text{where } \|\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|_2 = \|\hat{\mathbf{v}}\|_2 = 1$$
2. Attention-Mask-Aware Mean Pooling for BioBERT

Vanilla BioBERT produces token hidden states $h_i \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$. To avoid padding token distortion, we apply attention-mask-aware mean pooling:

$$\mathbf{e} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^L m_i \cdot \mathbf{h}_i}{\max\left(\sum_{i=1}^L m_i, \epsilon\right)}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{e}} = \frac{\mathbf{e}}{\|\mathbf{e}\|_2}$$
3. Jaccard & Normalized Levenshtein Metrics
$$J(A, B) = \frac{|A \cap B|}{|A \cup B|}, \quad \text{Sim}_{\text{Lev}}(s_1, s_2) = 1.0 - \frac{\text{LevenshteinDistance}(s_1, s_2)}{\max(|s_1|, |s_2|)}$$
