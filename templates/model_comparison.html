Why Transformers Outperform Lexical Methods
  • Synonym Invariance: Dense vectors represent conceptual meaning rather than character sequences, capturing clinical equivalents like "Stroke" and "Cerebrovascular Accident" despite 0% Jaccard overlap.
  • Contextual Polysemy: Understands words with multiple medical vs colloquial meanings depending on context (e.g. "Cold" symptom vs low temperature).
  • Latent Pathophysiological Associations: Embedding spaces cluster related organ systems and disease-symptom relationships together (e.g., "Hyperglycemia" and "Diabetes").
Technical Limitations & Edge Cases
  • Negation Sensitivity: Standard mean-pooled embeddings may score "Hypertension" and "No hypertension" moderately high due to token co-occurrence unless contrastive negation tuning is applied.
  • Subtle Clinical Distinction: Conditions sharing anatomical location (e.g., "Type 1" vs "Type 2 Diabetes" or "Gastric Ulcer" vs "Duodenal Ulcer") exhibit high semantic similarity while requiring radically different clinical treatment protocols.
  • Computational Footprint: Transformers require matrix multiplications and model weights (hundreds of megabytes), whereas Levenshtein/Jaccard execute in microseconds with zero memory overhead.
